## Dead coins

### 1. BitConnect (BCC) — Ponzi
- Peak: $463, December 2017 (some sources quote $509 in January 2018; use $463 Dec 2017).
- RIP: January 2018 — shut down its lending platform after cease-and-desist orders from Texas and North Carolina.
- Story: promised up to 40% per month via a "volatility trading bot"; ~$2.4B fraud; founder later charged by the SEC.
- Lesson: guaranteed returns are the reddest flag there is.
- Sources: thewhiteboxcrypto.substack.com/p/anatomy-of-a-listing; zmtacademy.com; thealphafactory.io/scam-report.

### 2. Terra Luna (LUNA) — depeg / death spiral
- Peak: $119.18, 5 April 2022.
- RIP: May 2022 — UST lost its $1 peg, death spiral, ~$40B in market value destroyed within days; LUNA fell to ~$0.00008 by June.
- Story: algorithmic stablecoin UST was "too big to fail" until it wasn't; the burn-and-mint peg accelerated the collapse once confidence broke.
- Lesson: an algorithmic peg is only as strong as the confidence behind it.
- Sources: thewhiteboxcrypto.substack.com/p/anatomy-of-a-listing; bitbank.nz/blog/crypto-graveyard; milkroad.com/crashes.

### 3. FTX Token (FTT) — exchange failure / fraud
- Peak: $84.18, September 2021.
- RIP: November 2022 — FTX filed for bankruptcy after customer funds were misused; FTT fell from ~$22 to under $1 within days.
- Story: one of the world's largest exchanges; founder directed billions in customer deposits to Alameda Research; Binance's announcement it would sell FTT triggered the bank run.
- Lesson: an exchange token is only as safe as the exchange's books — and you can't see the books.
- Sources: thewhiteboxcrypto.substack.com/p/anatomy-of-a-listing; bitbank.nz/blog/crypto-graveyard; zmtacademy.com.

### 4. Squid Game token (SQUID) — rug pull
- Peak: $2,861.80, 1 November 2021 (up ~75,000% in days).
- RIP: 1 November 2021 — developers drained ~$3.38M in liquidity in 5 minutes; price fell to ~$0.0008.
- Story: the smart contract had an anti-sell function so holders could not sell; only the developers could exit.
- Lesson: if you can't sell, you don't own an investment — you own a trap.
- Sources: thewhiteboxcrypto.substack.com/p/anatomy-of-a-listing; zmtacademy.com; thealphafactory.io/scam-report.

### 5. OneCoin (ONE) — Ponzi / fraud
- Peak: no real market (sold in packages, not traded); ~$4B taken from victims.
- RIP: exposed 2017–2019 as a fraud with no real blockchain behind it.
- Story: sold "educational packages" with tokens that could not be traded anywhere; one of the largest crypto frauds ever.
- Lesson: a coin you can't trade on any exchange isn't a coin.
- Sources: zmtacademy.com; bitbank.nz/blog/crypto-graveyard.

### 6. Celsius (CEL) — lending freeze / bankruptcy
- Peak: $8.05, June 2021.
- RIP: June–July 2022 — froze withdrawals, then filed for bankruptcy; founder later charged.
- Story: promised high yields on deposits; lent customer funds into risky bets that failed in the 2022 crash.
- Lesson: "earn yield on your crypto" means someone else is gambling with it.
- Sources: bitbank.nz/blog/crypto-graveyard.
